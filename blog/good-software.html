What made software design good

“The most clever piece of software will get re-written if no one understands how it works or how to work with it”.— My first programming teacher

10 years of projects, and I can’t begin listing all the times I’ve seen software written by brilliant people that rarely anyone could understand or even want to understand, let alone work with. Like an abandoned garden invaded by opportunistic weeds, I found most software systems will grind to a halt without tending to them.

So I don’t think it’s a coincidence that we would use words like clean, simple, and elegant to describe code we enjoyed working with.

Metaphors aside, I think what we were trying to say was simply that good code was written in such a way that it made it very easy for our minds to understand and contribute to. Endless grueling hours were spent trying to come up with a design that was neatly organized, well structured, while also providing space to grow wild ideas.

I think this is what made software a very unique discipline, where what constituted good was less about the efficiency or cleverness of our solutions, and more about the clarity of what we were trying to get done.

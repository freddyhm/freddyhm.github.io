What made good software good

“The most clever piece of software will get re-written if no one understands how it works or how to work with it”.

That was what my programming teacher said to us in my intro to programming class. For the next 20 years, those words proved to be more insightful than any tech conference I ever attended.

I can’t begin listing all the times I’ve seen software written by brilliant people that rarely anyone could understand or even want to understand, let alone tend to. Like an abandoned garden, entropy will eventually do its thing and the system will succumb to its own version of heat death.

Clean, simple, elegant were these nebulous adjectives engineers often used to describe code they enjoyed reading and working with, as opposed to like bloated, clunky, and complex.

Artsy metaphors aside, I think what we were trying to say was simply that these machine instructions are configured in such a way that it’s very easy for my brain to understand and it makes me want to contribute.

I think this is what makes software a very unique discipline, where what constitutes good seems to be less about how efficient our solutions are, and more about how clearly we communicate, not only to the computer, but to our peers.
